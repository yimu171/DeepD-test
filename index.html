Reading and writing genomes
across all domains of life

DeepD is a sparse mixture-of-experts genome foundation model that learns from genes and the full intergenic landscape across all domains of life. It combines single-nucleotide resolution with a million-token context and efficient sparse computation, supporting broad sequence-to-function prediction with particular strength in regulatory and non-coding tasks. Beyond experimentally validated promoter design, its Context–Anchor–Body (CAB) strategy enables controllable generation of entire yeast chromosomes and megabase-scale plant genomic segments. These sequences preserve coherent genic regions while introducing variations consistent with natural population patterns.

Introducing DeepD

One model to predict, interpret and design genomes

Genomes are long, heterogeneous and evolutionarily diverse, and most of their sequence is non-coding. DeepD was built to model them as they occur in nature. It pairs efficient sparse computation with pretraining on unselected whole genomes and a new Context–Anchor–Body strategy for controllable long-sequence generation.

DeepD was trained at two scales: DeepD-2B (1.8 billion parameters, 1 trillion tokens) and DeepD-60B (63 billion parameters, 5.69 trillion tokens). The same model supports zero-shot and supervised sequence-to-function prediction, exposes interpretable genomic features through its expert routing, and generates experimentally validated promoters, complete yeast chromosomes and megabase-scale Arabidopsis segments.

Efficient long context

A sparse mixture-of-experts backbone routes each nucleotide to only 8 of 576 experts, extending context from 8K to 1M tokens at a fraction of dense-model cost.

Phylogeny-informed

Species labels embedded from an established phylogenetic tree condition a species-informed router, so DeepD learns lineage-specific composition, codon usage and coding rules.

Unselected whole genomes

Pretraining keeps every genic region and the full intergenic space, including regulatory and repetitive DNA, rather than enriching for genes or sampling short segments.

Controllable generation

The Context–Anchor–Body strategy generates each segment between upstream and downstream constraints and relays forward, decoupling output length from the context window.

01 · Model

A sparse, phylogeny-aware architecture

DeepD integrates DNA sequence, species label embeddings and annotation features in a sparse mixture-of-experts transformer, trained with next-token prediction on byte-level nucleotides.

  • Species-informed router. The gating network sees both the token and a species representation, so experts can capture sequence-level and lineage-specific signals.
  • Multi-scale gated attention. In each block 24 of 32 heads use sliding-window attention and 8 use full attention, capturing local motifs and long-range dependencies. A sigmoid output gate stabilizes long-context training.
  • 576 experts, 8 active. One shared expert plus routed experts. Each token activates about 1 billion of the 63 billion parameters.
  • Reweighted objective. Soft-masked repeats stay in their native context but contribute less to the loss, so abundant repeats do not dominate optimization.

Species-informed routing

Pretrained on whole genomes from every domain of life

The DeepD-60B corpus comprises about 5.90 Tb of nucleotide sequence. The eukaryotic partition covers 3,600 animal, 928 plant, 767 fungal and 139 protist genomes. Coding sequence, introns, repeats, other intergenic DNA and viral genomes are all represented, so the model learns genome-wide organization rather than genes alone.

Long context, efficiently

Pretraining runs at an 8K context. Midtraining then extends the window step by step to 1M tokens. Validation perplexity keeps improving as the context grows, and sparse routing keeps the cost of ultra-long sequences manageable.

02 · Interpretability

What DeepD learns about genomes

DeepD's experts organize themselves around biology. Probing them reveals species-specific coding rules, and routing weights alone are enough to decode genes, repeats and distal regulatory elements.

Phylogeny-informed modeling captures species-specific rules

Early layers contain distinct AT-activated and GC-activated expert clusters. Species tags then steer generation toward each lineage's GC content and codon usage. Zero-shot likelihoods also respect synonymous codon preferences and penalize disruptive mutations.

Expert routing interprets coding, regulatory and repetitive DNA

We trained lightweight classifiers on DeepD's 576-dimensional gating vectors from layers 2–24. Routing alone resolves CRISPR arrays and operons in E. coli, gene structure and repeat classes in human (mean AUPRC 0.92), and distal enhancers in the repeat-rich maize genome.

03 · Prediction

Sequence-to-function prediction across species

DeepD scores variants zero-shot by the change in sequence likelihood, with no task-specific labels. Its embeddings also support supervised prediction of regulatory function. It is strongest where genomes are hardest to read: non-coding and regulatory DNA.

Human variant effects and regulatory DNA

Across five zero-shot human benchmarks DeepD reaches AUROCs of 0.814–0.965. It is competitive with the Evo 2 series and Nucleotide Transformer (NT) v2, and outperforms AlphaGenome on protein fitness, ClinVar, BRCA1 and BRCA2, with particular strength on synonymous, splice-related and non-coding variants.

      04 · Design

      Controllable design, from promoters to chromosomes

      DeepD moves generation from open-ended continuation toward specification. It writes regulatory elements with a requested strength and builds chromosome-scale sequences that keep gene structure and order while varying within natural ranges.

      Maize core promoters with specified strength, validated in vivo

      Fine-tuned on STARR-seq activity labels, DeepD generates novel 170-bp synthetic core promoters conditioned on High or Low activity. The designs are dissimilar to natural maize sequences, yet dual-luciferase assays in maize protoplasts confirm the intended direction.

      Context–Anchor–Body

      Generating beyond a single context window

      Early errors in autoregressive generation compound with length. CAB instead writes each 8-kb Body between an upstream Anchor and a downstream Context, then relays forward: the freshly generated segment anchors the next round. Total output length is no longer tied to the training window.

      Complete yeast chromosomes

      Fine-tuned on 762 S. cerevisiae genomes with the S288C reference held out, DeepD generated 29 full-length chromosome III sequences on the S288C backbone. They keep reference-like gene content and order while carrying variation that mirrors the natural yeast pangenome.
